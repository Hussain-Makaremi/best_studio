# SaaS App Design Prompts

Work software demands visual systems that endure repeated use. Three approaches for designing SaaS interfaces.

> Note: the fetch returned this page in condensed form; no bracketed prompt template was returned for it.

## Direction 1 — Focused workspaces

"Give the task a stable frame and make the next action visible."

Examples: Obsidian, Letters, Bento, Wrike, Tatem, Superlist

### Obsidian

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Graphite | #3f3f3f |
| Bright Gray | #eeeeee |
| White | #ffffff |
| Medium Gray | #bcbcbc |

Typography: ui-sans-serif, system-ui

Guidance: choose contrast levels that keep navigation, current work, and available actions distinct during extended sessions.

## Direction 2 — Content-led products

"When documents, messages, or tasks are the product, show the material people will work with."

Examples: ClickUp™, Lazy, Slite, Slack, Mesh, Lattice

### ClickUp™

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Signal White | #ffffff |
| Ink Black | #202020 |
| Onyx | #090c1d |
| Carbon | #2a2a2a |

Typography: Plus Jakarta Sans · Inter

Guidance: real lists, text, and statuses should inform density decisions before establishing headline scale.

## Direction 3 — Friendly operations

Warmth can lower visual intensity without sacrificing structural clarity.

Examples: boords.com, Micro, Pitch, Dock, Workable, Contractbook

### boords.com

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Warm Cream | #fafaf5 |
| Ink Black | #121212 |
| Stone Gray | #e9e9e7 |
| Pure White | #ffffff |

Typography: matter · ui-monospace

Guidance: use expressive visual layers for introductions while keeping repeated controls and content areas predictable.
