# Settings Page Design Prompts

A settings area inherits the product's visual system. These references offer three ways to separate controls and supporting information: cool cards, dark layers, or welcoming color zones.

## Prompt: Make each setting easy to understand

The style document supplies the visual rules. Fill in the bracketed product facts before giving this task to an agent.

```
Design settings for [product] where [role] manages [actual preferences and permissions].
Use the linked Quizlet DESIGN.md for its blue-white canvas, white surfaces, and restrained
violet emphasis. Group routine preferences apart from account and risky changes. Show current
values, save behavior, access limits, and confirmation or undo from our product rules.
```

## Direction 1 — Cool canvas, clear cards (Quizlet)

The #f6f7fb canvas, white cards, and Hurme Geometric Sans suggest a quiet structure for preferences: group related controls by surface before adding more borders or colors.

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|

| Pure Black | #000000 |
| Fog Mist | #939bb4 |
| Ink Charcoal | #282e3e |

Typography: Hurme Geometric Sans

Related: Replit (warm workshop with coral sparks), Campsite (sunlit editorial workspace), Reflect Notes (starlit violet cosmos), Clay (bright playroom of clay shapes), Todoist (sunlit workspace with paper aesthetic).

## Direction 2 — Layers inside a dark workspace (FlutterFlow)

The #060311 canvas and #161320 surfaces do most of the grouping. Urbanist provides the broad brand voice; compact settings labels can stay quiet inside those layers.

## Direction 3 — A friendlier account area (Loom)

Charlie Display handles the headline while Charlie Text stays readable in smaller UI. Its #1868db accent and pale blue #e9f2fe surface show how an account area can feel warm without giving every control a new color.
